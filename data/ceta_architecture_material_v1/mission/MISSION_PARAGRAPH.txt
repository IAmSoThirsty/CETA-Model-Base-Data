CETA's intended job is to choose the next warranted transition over explicit epistemic, identity, authority, and action state so that increasingly capable AI systems can work beside humans as bounded guardians and stewards of Earth: protecting people, ecosystems, infrastructure, knowledge, consent, and continuity without confusing intelligence with authority. Its decisions are meant to sit between observation/language/tool inputs and governed state change, where they can guide research, planning, verification, authorization requests, recovery, and—only through a separate effect gateway—real-world action. CETA must never invent evidence, identity, consent, scope, authority, verification, or successful effects; it must never convert a mission, role title, confidence score, or legal-looking proposal into self-granted permission; and it must never become the sole executor and verifier of its own consequential action. The most dangerous failure is a false allow at the authority/effect boundary—especially Authorize, ExpandScope, Execute, or Rollback—where forged, stale, incomplete, or mis-scoped state is treated as sufficient and an irreversible human or environmental consequence is both caused and incorrectly recorded as justified.
